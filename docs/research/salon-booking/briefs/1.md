# Brief 1: a customer who books in the evening

**Who they are.** A 28-year-old who works in an office and commutes by train. They get a haircut about every eight weeks at a small local salon, usually with the same stylist. Their phone is always with them, but they almost never use it for calls.

**What they do today.** They remember they need a haircut in the evening, on the sofa or on the train home, when the salon is closed. They put off calling the next day because they're at their desk and don't want colleagues to hear, so it sometimes slips for a week or two. When they finally ring during a lunch break, it sometimes goes unanswered and they don't leave a voicemail. Once they gave up and went to a different salon that had a free slot the same week.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- In one booking-software firm's study of 5,000 salons, 28% of bookings were made in the evening after closing and 18% in the morning before opening. Even daytime bookings were often made "on the go, or in busy workplaces": `sources/phorest-after-hours.md`, paragraphs 6 and 9.
- In the same firm's survey, 36% of 25 to 34 year olds want to book through a salon's website: `sources/phorest-after-hours.md`, paragraph 5.
- Almost 60% of Gen Z Australians in a 2023 survey dread making or taking a phone call, and 81% of US millennials in another survey feel anxious before making one: `sources/phone-anxiety.md`, paragraph 17.
- In a salon software firm's data, only 24% of callers who reach no one leave a voicemail: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraph 55. In its survey, 71% of regulars had skipped booking at some point because it was too hard to reach someone: paragraph 10.
