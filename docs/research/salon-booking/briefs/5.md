Role: an edge case

# Brief 5: a customer with hearing loss who can't book by phone

**Who they are.** A 63-year-old retired librarian with severe hearing loss in both ears. They wear hearing aids and lip-read well face to face, but phone calls are close to impossible. They have a tablet at home but find small screens and fiddly forms on a phone hard, because their eyesight isn't what it was.

**What they do today.** They walk to the salon to book in person, often when it is busy, and wait until a stylist is free to look at the book. If they need to change a booking, they ask a relative to phone for them, which they dislike, or they turn up and ask. They once tried a relay service to call a business, but the call was slow and the person who answered hung up, thinking it was a sales call. They would like confirmations in writing, so they can be sure of the date and time.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- Phone calls are a vital tool for 80% of the deaf community and people with difficulty speaking, but poor experiences leave callers frustrated and dependent on others. Millions of people with hearing loss in Britain struggle to call businesses on their own: `sources/relay-uk-business.md`, paragraph 38.
- Businesses are urged to learn how such customers try to reach them, for example through the Relay UK text relay service: `sources/relay-uk-business.md`, paragraphs 39 and 40.
- Salon Today says the pandemic made online tools a more everyday thing "for older generations, as well": `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 41.
- Booking tools commonly send customers an email confirmation and reminder: `sources/salonized-guide.md`, paragraph 43.
