# Brief 6: a regular who books at the till and phones if anything changes

**Who they are.** A 72-year-old retired bus driver who has had a cut and set with the same stylist every four weeks for nine years. They own a basic mobile phone for calls and texts, no smartphone, and don't use the internet at home.

**What they do today.** At the end of each appointment they book the next one at the desk, and the stylist writes it on a little card that goes on the fridge. If something comes up, they phone the salon, and if nobody answers they ring again later or just pop in, since it's on their way to the shops. They enjoy the chat on the phone and at the desk, and see no reason to book any other way. They would never look up the salon online.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- In one salon-software firm's data, 57% of guests still book by phone: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraph 18.
- Many salons trusted customers to walk in, or drop by, without an appointment: `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 42.
- Salon staff spend their days with clients, juggling walk-ins, checkouts and the phone, and building relationships with regulars: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraph 12; `sources/bls-hairstylists.md`, paragraph 101.
- One booking-software firm's survey reports wanting to book online only for younger customers (43% of 18 to 24 year olds expect it, and 36% of 25 to 34 year olds want it). It gives no figure for older customers: `sources/phorest-after-hours.md`, paragraph 5.
