# Brief 3: a parent who books around shifts and children

**Who they are.** A 41-year-old nurse who works rotating shifts and has two children aged 9 and 13. They book haircuts for themselves and for the children, often together on a day off, and like one particular stylist for the younger child, who doesn't sit still easily.

**What they do today.** Their rota comes out a few weeks ahead, usually late at night after the children are in bed. That is when they want to book, but the salon is closed. On shift they can't take or make personal calls, so they try to ring on a day off, and then they have to say which stylist, which services and which day while the children talk over them. When a shift changes at short notice, they need to move a booking, and calling during the day isn't possible. More than once they have simply not turned up, and felt bad about it.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- In one booking-software firm's study, 46% of salon bookings were made while salons were closed, 28% of them in the evening after closing: `sources/phorest-after-hours.md`, paragraphs 4 and 6.
- In another firm's data, up to 34% of appointment requests come after hours, and 62% of regulars say they are more likely to choose a salon that offers after-hours support: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraphs 16 and 62.
- Clients who can't reach someone to change or cancel an appointment can end up as no-shows: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraph 25.
- Booking tools record a service, a stylist (the employee), a date and a time for each appointment, and email the customer a confirmation and a reminder: `sources/salonized-guide.md`, paragraphs 27 and 43.
