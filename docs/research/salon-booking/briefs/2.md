# Brief 2: a stylist who answers the phone between clients

**Who they are.** A 34-year-old stylist, one of three at a two-chair salon with no receptionist. They have cut hair for twelve years and have a loyal set of regulars. They work most Saturdays and two late evenings a week.

**What they do today.** The salon's diary is a paper book at the front desk, plus a shared phone. When the phone rings mid-colour or mid-cut, they either let it ring or put down the scissors, dry their hands and answer. They then check the book for the right stylist's free time, write the booking in pencil, and go back to the client. Regulars also text their personal mobile to book, which they answer in the evening and copy into the book the next morning. Sometimes two people write in the same slot. They return missed calls at the end of the day, when many callers have already booked somewhere else.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- In one software firm's data, 37% of calls to salons and spas go unanswered, and 82% of those are missed during opening hours, while staff are busy with walk-ins, checkouts and guests in the salon: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraphs 9 and 12.
- A booking-tool CEO said: "It's mentally very demanding if you need to be picking up the phone all the time. Every missed call might be a loss of income": `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 40.
- A salon that kept its bookings with paper and pen said its work was "constantly stopped by the phone ringing". After moving to digital booking, it said it got 70% fewer calls a day and could take Sundays off: `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraphs 43, 44 and 46.
- Stylists' hours often include evenings and weekends, the busiest times, and managing time when scheduling appointments is a core skill of the job: `sources/bls-hairstylists.md`, paragraphs 86 and 105.
