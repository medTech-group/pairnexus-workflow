# Brief 4: a salon owner who has run the diary by phone for twenty years

**Who they are.** A 56-year-old owner who also cuts hair full time in their own small salon. They employ two other stylists, one part time and one renting a chair. They have kept the appointment book on paper since they opened, and know most of their clients by voice.

**What they do today.** They take bookings by phone, in person at the desk, and from regulars who drop in to ask. Many regulars book their next appointment at the till before they leave. They decide each booking themselves: how long a colour really takes for this client, who needs a longer slot, which new client to fit in, and when to keep a gap for a walk-in. They have been sold software before: a sales rep set up a system, the subscription went up, staff stopped using it, and they went back to the paper book. They think the figures about "lost calls" come from the people selling the fix.

**What people like them have said or done.**
- By one estimate from a booking-tool firm, 65% of European hair and beauty salons use no digital tool for appointments or payments: `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 38.
- Many salons once relied on customers walking in without an appointment: `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 42.
- Most UK hair and beauty businesses are tiny: more than 80% employ fewer than five people, and nearly 64% turn over less than £99k a year. Labour is about 60% of costs, and margins are tight: `sources/icaew-industry.md`, paragraphs 29, 31 and 54.
- One salon said it had worried its clients "wouldn't be able to use digital tools, as they were used to booking us through the phone" (in its case the worry proved wrong, as reported by a booking-tool firm): `sources/salon-today-booking-times.md`, paragraph 44.
- The missed-call figures that are easy to find come from a firm that sells an AI phone receptionist, and the same firm says 57% of guests still book by phone: `sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md`, paragraphs 3 and 18.
