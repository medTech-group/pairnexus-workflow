# A small salon's booking page: who misses out on booking today, and what would a page have to get right?

The question: should a small hair salon's booking page let customers pick a service, a stylist and a time, and send the salon a confirmation? The salon, the product and the personas are made up; the sources cited are real public pages, saved beside this note. The intake on #593 framed the study: for the salon's customers and its staff; the problem is booking calls missed while staff are with clients, and no way to book after hours; success is fewer missed calls and bookings made after hours, over the page's first 3 months, against before (the figures set in grilling); no payments or deposits, and no customer accounts.

Every finding carries one label. *Observed (public source)*: a saved copy says it, named with its paragraph. *Simulated*: it rests only on the six made-up personas' answers (`transcripts/`) or the panel's reasoning. No finding is *observed (real person)*: the owner brought in nothing.

**Read with care.** Most figures on missed calls and on when people book come from firms that sell booking or call-answering software (Zenoti, Phorest, Book Salon): Zenoti's page is about its AI receptionist ([Zenoti](sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md), paragraph 3). The panel found no independent survey of missed calls at small salons (*simulated*: the evidence gatherer's search). The personas' briefs were written from the same evidence, so where a persona agrees with it, the brief put it there.

## Findings from public sources

1. Most UK hair and beauty businesses are very small: as of 2024, nearly 64% turned over less than £99k a year, more than 80% employed fewer than five people and 95% fewer than ten (*observed (public source)*; [the ICAEW industry profile](sources/icaew-industry.md), paragraph 29).
2. 58% of people working in UK hair and beauty are self-employed, many running a single owner-managed salon or renting a chair or room in someone else's (*observed (public source)*; [ICAEW](sources/icaew-industry.md), paragraphs 30 and 59). In the US, 48% of hairdressers, hairstylists and cosmetologists are self-employed (*observed (public source)*; [the BLS outlook](sources/bls-hairstylists.md), paragraphs 79 and 80), and 80% of barbers (paragraphs 76 and 77); some lease booth space from a salon owner (paragraph 83).
3. Inflation, above all in employment costs, has left many salon businesses with tight profit margins, and labour is about 60% of their expenses (*observed (public source)*; [ICAEW](sources/icaew-industry.md), paragraphs 31 and 54).
4. Stylists' schedules often include evenings and weekends, when salons may be busiest, and managing time when scheduling appointments is named as a core skill of the job (*observed (public source)*; [BLS](sources/bls-hairstylists.md), paragraphs 86 and 105).
5. The phone is still the main way to book: in Zenoti's data, 57% of guests still book by phone (*observed (public source)*; [Zenoti](sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md), paragraph 18).
6. In Zenoti's data, 37% of calls to salons and spas are missed, 82% of them during business hours and 18% after, and only 24% of callers leave a voicemail (*observed (public source)*; [Zenoti](sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md), paragraph 55). So in the only missed-call data found, most missed calls happen while the salon is open.
7. Zenoti says 71% of salon and spa regulars have skipped booking because it was too hard to reach someone (*observed (public source)*; [Zenoti](sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md), paragraph 10), and that clients who can't reach anyone to change or cancel end up as no-shows (paragraph 25). The page gives no source or year for the 71%.
8. Many salons that once trusted customers to walk in without an appointment have changed, and without a digital system it is hard to fill no-shows and last-minute cancellations (*observed (public source)*; [Salon Today](sources/salon-today-booking-times.md), paragraph 42).
9. Answering the phone between clients wears on staff: a booking-tool CEO calls it "mentally very demanding" (*observed (public source)*; [Salon Today](sources/salon-today-booking-times.md), paragraph 40), and one salon that kept bookings on paper says its work "was constantly stopped by the phone ringing" (paragraph 43).
10. Phorest analysed the booking times of 5,000 salons and spas and surveyed 1,000 customers: 46% of bookings were made while salons were closed, 28% in the evening and 18% before opening (*observed (public source)*; [the Phorest report](sources/phorest-after-hours.md), paragraphs 4 and 6). Phorest adds that even the 54% made during working hours happen "on the go, or in busy workplaces" (paragraph 9).
11. Book Salon's data from its client portfolio of more than 1,000 salons shows 52% of bookings happen between 5 PM and 9 AM (*observed (public source)*; [Salon Today](sources/salon-today-booking-times.md), paragraph 37).
12. Hairdressers Journal reported in November 2023 that a majority of British salon appointments are now made outside opening hours, via online booking systems (*observed (public source)*; [ICAEW](sources/icaew-industry.md), paragraph 69).
13. Zenoti puts it lower: up to 34% of appointment requests come after hours (*observed (public source)*; [Zenoti](sources/zenoti-missed-calls.md), paragraph 16), and 62% of regulars are more likely to choose a salon that offers after-hours support (paragraph 62). The sources measure different things, requests or bookings, so the share outside hours runs from about a third to a half (*simulated*: the panel's reasoning).
14. In Phorest's data, 43% of 18 to 24 year olds expect salons to offer online booking, and 36% of 25 to 34 year olds want to book online via a salon's website (*observed (public source)*; [Phorest](sources/phorest-after-hours.md), paragraph 5). It gives no figure for older customers.
15. Almost 60% of Gen Z Australians in a 2023 survey dread making or taking a phone call, and 81% of US millennials in another get anxious before making one (*observed (public source)*; [The Conversation](sources/phone-anxiety.md), paragraph 17). Telephone anxiety goes with a weaker preference for calls, so choosing another channel may be about escaping anxiety, not only convenience (paragraph 43).
16. Phone calls are a vital tool for 80% of the deaf community and people who have difficulty speaking, but poor experiences leave callers frustrated and dependent on others (*observed (public source)*; [the Relay UK business toolkit](sources/relay-uk-business.md), paragraph 38).
17. Salon Today says the pandemic made online tools a more everyday activity "for older generations, as well" (*observed (public source)*; [Salon Today](sources/salon-today-booking-times.md), paragraph 41). One salon that worried its phone-booking clients couldn't use digital tools now gets 70% fewer calls a day (paragraph 44): one salon's account, told by a booking firm.
18. "According to some estimates", 65% of European beauty and hair salons use no digital tool for appointments or payments (*observed (public source)*; [Salon Today](sources/salon-today-booking-times.md), paragraph 38). The estimate's source isn't given.
19. In one small-salon tool, Salonized, each appointment records the service, duration, employee, date and time (*observed (public source)*; [the Salonized guide](sources/salonized-guide.md), paragraph 27); it emails customers a confirmation and a reminder (paragraph 43); and its booking widget lets customers book themselves, on the salon's own website or Facebook page (paragraphs 54 and 57).

## Findings from the panel

Every finding here is *simulated*: it rests on the made-up personas' answers, read by the synthesizer. Where an answer was the persona's own guess, it says so.

1. **The need to book comes when nobody can answer, so booking slips rather than always being lost.** The evening booker takes about two weeks from "I need a haircut" to a booked slot (*simulated*; [transcript 1](transcripts/1.md), Q1, partly a guess), and the shift-working parent can only ring on days off (*simulated*; [transcript 3](transcripts/3.md), Q1 and Q4).
2. **Staff can't answer while they are with a client.** The stylist lets the phone ring mid-colour, or loses five or six minutes taking gloves off to answer (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q1 and Q3); the owner lets it ring out (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q3).
3. **Changing a booking may be a bigger problem than making one.** The parent misses appointments because a shift changed and there was no way to tell the salon; the reminder isn't the problem, telling them is (*simulated*; [transcript 3](transcripts/3.md), Q5, Q6 and Q11). The stylist says a same-day cancellation usually leaves an empty chair (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q5), while the owner fills one with a walk-in (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q5).
4. **People book a person, not a slot.** Every persona ties the booking to one stylist (*simulated*; Q2 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md) to [6](transcripts/6.md)). Every persona agreeing is a warning: see below.
5. **A booking feels real once there is something in writing, straight away.** Three customers reject a "request received" confirmed the next day (*simulated*; Q10 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md), [3](transcripts/3.md) and [5](transcripts/5.md)); the customer with hearing loss wants the date in words, not "14/11" (*simulated*; [transcript 5](transcripts/5.md), Q10).
6. **Staff need one diary they can trust.** The stylist wants "one diary, not the book plus my phone plus a website", and names double bookings and texts not copied into the book (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q10 and Q11); for the owner a booking anywhere but the day page "might as well not exist" (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q10).
7. **How long an appointment takes depends on the client, and staff judge it.** A full colour and a fringe trim differ, "a cut" can turn out to be a restyle, and new clients get a longer slot (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q9; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q9).
8. **"Safe" means the right person changed it and the salon saw it.** Customers fear someone else cancelling, and staff fear a change they don't see before the day (*simulated*; Q7 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md), [2](transcripts/2.md), [3](transcripts/3.md) and [4](transcripts/4.md)).
9. **The phone and the desk have to stay.** The regular with no smartphone would feel "pushed out" if told to go online (*simulated*; [transcript 6](transcripts/6.md), Q12, partly a guess).
10. **Bookings already come through channels nobody counts.** Regulars text the stylist's own mobile, answered at 10pm and copied into the book next morning "if I remember" (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q3), and book the next visit at the till (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q1; [transcript 6](transcripts/6.md), Q1).
11. **Nobody wants an app for one salon.** (*simulated*; Q8 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md), [5](transcripts/5.md) and [6](transcripts/6.md).)

## Disagreements

- **Who controls the diary.** Customers who book outside a call want a slot confirmed at once (*simulated*; Q10 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md) and [3](transcripts/3.md)); the owner would want to approve each booking, keeps gaps for walk-ins and doesn't want "people moving themselves around in my day" (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q5 and Q10, the approving a guess).
- **Whether missed calls are a real loss.** The stylist says half the end-of-day callbacks have booked elsewhere (*simulated*; [transcript 2](transcripts/2.md), Q3); the owner doesn't believe figures "from the people selling the answering machine" and has never counted (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q3). The only figures are Zenoti's (finding 6 above).
- **When people book.** The owner says most bookings happen at the till (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q4); the public figures say a third to a half happen outside opening hours (findings 10 to 13 above). Book Salon's figure comes from its own clients' bookings, and Hairdressers Journal's from online booking systems, so at least those two measure salons already taking bookings online; how much of a paper-book salon's trade would move outside hours is unknown (*simulated*: the panel's reasoning).
- **How to prove it's you.** A code sent to the phone reassures the evening booker (*simulated*; [transcript 1](transcripts/1.md), Q7) but locks out the customer with hearing loss if it comes as a voice call or must be typed into a small box (*simulated*; [transcript 5](transcripts/5.md), Q7), and the regular finds codes and links "not for me" (*simulated*; [transcript 6](transcripts/6.md), Q7).
- **Showing the price.** Customers want it up front (*simulated*; Q9 of [transcripts 1](transcripts/1.md) and [5](transcripts/5.md)); staff say it varies by client (*simulated*; Q9 of [transcripts 2](transcripts/2.md) and [4](transcripts/4.md)).

## Surprises

- In the only missed-call data, most missed calls come while the salon is open (finding 6 above): the larger pain for staff is interruption mid-service, not the hours after closing (*simulated*: the panel's reasoning).
- One booking can be three people: a parent books herself and two children back to back, one with a fixed stylist, and wants to move all three at once (*simulated*; [transcript 3](transcripts/3.md), Q7 and Q9).
- One salon can hold several books: the chair renter "keeps her own clients" (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q2).
- The owner's last booking software failed because staff stopped using it and its price rose (*simulated*; [transcript 4](transcripts/4.md), Q8 and Q11): adoption inside the salon may be the bigger risk.
- A missed relay call: the customer with hearing loss once called through a relay service and was hung up on as a sales call (*simulated*; [transcript 5](transcripts/5.md), Q3, from the persona's brief, not from a source).

## Agreement as a warning

- All six personas tie the booking to one stylist. Brief 6 describes a regular of nine years, and question 2 asks how they choose a stylist, which assumes there is a choice; no walk-in customer or barbershop is on the panel (*simulated*: the synthesizer's reading).
- The personas who explain missed calls agree they go unanswered while staff are busy, but their briefs quote the same vendor's page (*simulated*: the synthesizer's reading).
- Question 7 starts from the no-account design, so the answers on safety are about that design, not about whether customers would want an account (*simulated*: the synthesizer's reading).
- The panel is narrow: no new client, no salon already using booking software, no salon with a receptionist, and the chair renter only described by the owner (*simulated*: the synthesizer's reading).

## Ranked questions

Every question a real person or a real test would most change, most important first. Each rests on *simulated* findings above.

1. Do small salons that keep a paper book lose bookings to unanswered calls and messages, and how many? Settle it: two or three real salons log every missed call and message for an ordinary week, and whether the caller booked in the end.
2. Would staff keep one shared diary up to date for three months without being nagged? Settle it: a real salon's account of the last tool it dropped, then a trial counting staff entries each week.
3. Will an owner let customers take a confirmed slot without approving it, and on what terms? Settle it: ask real owners which slots, services and clients they would open, and which they would hold back for walk-ins.
4. Who sets how long an appointment takes, and can a form get it right? Settle it: compare a real salon's last month of bookings with the lengths a fixed service list would give.
5. Does letting people change or cancel without a call cut no-shows, or turn them into late cancellations? Settle it: in a trial, count no-shows and cancellations inside 24 hours, before and after.
6. In a salon that doesn't take bookings online yet, how much booking would move outside opening hours, and is it regulars or newcomers? Settle it: a trial logging each booking's time and whether the client is new.
7. Can someone with hearing loss, someone with poor eyesight and an older customer who isn't online each confirm, change and cancel a booking without a call, or keep their old way untouched? Settle it: try a mock-up's change and cancel steps with real people.
8. How do real customers prove it's them without an account, and what feels unsafe? Settle it: ask real customers an open question that doesn't start from the no-account design.
9. How does a booking made by phone, at the till or by text reach the one diary, and who does that work? Settle it: watch one real salon's desk for a working day.
10. What does a small salon already use, and why would it change? Settle it: ask real salons that use a booking tool what they pay and miss, and the ones that use none why not.
11. What would a salon pay, who pays (owner or chair renter), and how does it work for a chair renter's own book?
12. Would an owner rather have no software, a better phone or a part-time receptionist?
13. How many regulars book by text to a stylist's own phone, and would they accept something else?
14. Is booking a particular stylist as universal as the panel says, and would seeing that stylist's free times keep a customer from going elsewhere?
15. How do staff hear about a new booking or a change without being interrupted mid-service, and in time to act on it?
16. Does booking at any hour bring regulars in more often?
17. What confirmation do people need, in what channel, and who pays for texts?
18. How should one booking for a family be made and moved?
19. Which services can't be booked straight away (patch tests, consultations, big changes), and how is the price given when it varies?
20. How should a lasting note about a client ("hard of hearing, message don't call") be kept so nobody repeats it?
21. Does the reminder matter, or only the ability to change?
22. What happens when the internet or the device is down on a busy Saturday?
23. What happens to the client list, and the personal details in it (children's included), if the salon stops paying?
24. Should a booking carry a cancellation policy? (Deposits are outside the intake's limits.)
25. How does a new client find the salon and book?
